LATE-ONSET BK POLYOMAVIRUS-ASSOCIATED NEPHROPATHY MANAGED BY EVEROLIMUS CONVERSION: A LONGITUDINAL CASE REPORT AND SYSTEMATIC REVIEW OF CONTEMPORARY EVIDENCE

Authors

Van Le Quy Dinh1, Hien Trong Nguyen1.

Affiliations

1 Urology, Cho Ray Hospital, Ho Chi Minh city, Viet Nam

Abstract

Background

BK polyomavirus-associated nephropathy (BKVAN) remains a major infectious threat to long-term kidney allograft survival, occurring in 2–16% of recipients and associated with graft loss rates of up to 50% when undetected. Optimal management strategies, particularly the role of mammalian target of rapamycin inhibitor (mTORi) conversion, remain debated following the publication of the BKEVER randomised trial.
Case Summary

We present the longitudinal clinical course of a 33-year-old male kidney transplant recipient who developed biopsy-confirmed, late-onset BKVAN approximately three years after deceased-donor kidney transplantation at Cho Ray Hospital, Vietnam.

Despite a relatively low plasma viral load at the time of biopsy (9.3 × 10² copies/mL), rising serum creatinine and positive SV40 immunohistochemistry prompted prompt immunosuppression restructuring, including discontinuation of mycophenolate mofetil and conversion to everolimus.

Following an initial post-conversion viral surge, progressive viral suppression culminated in complete clearance (BKPyV <600 copies/mL) by December 2025—three years after diagnosis—with stable graft function (serum creatinine 1.53 mg/dL; eGFR 59.3 mL/min/1.73 m²).
Conclusions

This case illustrates the diagnostic value of early allograft biopsy even at low plasma viral loads, and demonstrates that everolimus-based conversion can achieve durable viral clearance while preserving graft function. Contextualised against the ATHENA, TRANSFORM, Wiseman, and BKEVER trials and the 2024 International Consensus Guidelines, we discuss the evolving evidence for mTORi use in BKVAN and highlight practical constraints—including PCR access and cost—that influence management in resource-limited settings.
