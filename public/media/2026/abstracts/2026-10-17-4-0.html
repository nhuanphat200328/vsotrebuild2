ABSTRACT

The Korean experience in organ donation coordination

Curie Ahn, MD, PhD

Seoul National University


Korea’s organ transplant program began in 1969 with the first living donor kidney transplant. Transplant numbers in Korea have steadily increased. In 2023, over 4,000 transplants were performed, with 42% from deceased donors. Nonetheless, waitlists and wait times keep growing. Last year, almost 8 patients died daily while waiting. This desperation drives some patients to pursue illegal overseas transplants, risking their safety and compromising ethical standards.

Two milestones in developing a deceased donor transplant program are 1) Establishing legislative frameworks and 2) Creating a national agency.

Establishing the necessary structures and organizations is crucial to achieving self-sufficiency in deceased organ donation and transplantation. To support these efforts, we have adopted four operational requirements and three regulatory requirements, in line with WHO recommendations. These collective efforts demonstrate the importance of systematic development in achieving sustainable and ethical organ transplantation programs.

Through legislative initiatives, the establishment of a national transplantation organization, an independent Organ Procurement Organization (OPO), and the implementation of mandatory reporting, and the Vitallink, Korea has achieved a significant increase in deceased donors and a notable reduction in overseas transplants.
