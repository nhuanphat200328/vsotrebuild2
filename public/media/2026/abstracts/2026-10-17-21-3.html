Acute Rejection After Early Tacrolimus-to-Everolimus Conversion in a Kidney Transplant Recipient with Delayed Graft Function: A Case Report

Van Hieu Nguyen1; Ngoc Phuong Thao Huynh1,2; Minh Hoang Tran1,2

1. University Medical Center Ho Chi Minh City, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam

2. University of Medicine and Pharmacy at Ho Chi Minh City, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam

ABSTRACT

Background: Delayed graft function (DGF) poses a challenge in balancing calcineurin inhibitor (CNI)-associated nephrotoxicity against adequate immunosuppression after kidney transplantation. Evidence supporting complete CNI withdrawal during ongoing DGF remains limited.

Case presentation: A 19-year-old woman with kidney failure caused by focal segmental glomerulosclerosis underwent ABO-compatible living-related kidney transplantation. Pretransplant evaluation showed three of six human leukocyte antigen mismatches, 0% panel-reactive antibodies, and a negative crossmatch. Following basiliximab induction, maintenance immunosuppression consisted of tacrolimus, mycophenolate mofetil, and corticosteroids. Progressive oliguria followed by anuria developed despite normal graft ultrasonography. Tacrolimus was replaced with everolimus on postoperative day (POD) 18, followed by improved urine output and graft function. At discharge on POD 23, serum creatinine was 2.03 mg/dL and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was 36 mL/min/1.73 m². From POD 30, fatigue, intermittent low-grade fever, and progressive graft dysfunction developed, with eGFR declining to 13 mL/min/1.73 m² by POD 72. De novo donor-specific antibodies were detected (mean fluorescence intensity, 5,155). Allograft biopsy revealed Banff grade IIA T cell–mediated rejection, acute tubular injury, and C4d positivity in glomerular and peritubular capillaries, raising suspicion of concomitant antibody-mediated rejection. Treatment included corticosteroid pulses, five plasma exchange sessions, intravenous immunoglobulin, and antithymocyte globulin. Maintenance therapy was changed to cyclosporine, mycophenolate mofetil, and corticosteroids. At six months, eGFR had recovered to 51 mL/min/1.73 m².

Conclusion: This case report highlights the potential trade-off between improvement in allograft function and the risk of acute rejection following CNI withdrawal and conversion to everolimus during DGF. Close monitoring and timely allograft biopsy are essential when graft function deteriorates to establish the diagnosis and guide appropriate treatment.

Keywords: kidney transplantation; delayed graft function; everolimus; tacrolimus; acute rejection; allograft biopsy
