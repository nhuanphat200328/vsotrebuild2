ABSTRACT IN ENGLISH

UPDATED CONTRAINDICATIONS AND READINESS CRITERIA FOR ANESTHESIA AND KIDNEY TRANSPLANTATION IN RECIPIENTS

Pham Van Dong, MD, PhD

Department of Anesthesiology and Surgical Services, Cho Ray Hospital, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam

Background: Preoperative assessment of kidney transplant recipients requires a clear distinction between true contraindications and potentially correctable conditions to avoid unnecessary transplantation delays or exclusions.

Objective: To review updated contraindications, readiness criteria, and perioperative anesthesia safety considerations in kidney transplant recipients.

Main Content: This narrative review synthesizes recommendations from Barash’s Clinical Anesthesia (2024), Miller’s Anesthesia Review (2025), and Kidney Transplantation: Principles and Practice (2026). Key considerations include infection, electrolyte and acid–base disturbances, fluid overload, cardiovascular and vascular disease, coagulation abnormalities, anemia, malignancy, immune-mediated disorders, HIV infection, and treatment adherence. Emphasis is placed on individualized risk assessment, preoperative optimization, and prevention of residual neuromuscular blockade to facilitate safe postoperative tracheal extubation.

Conclusion: Clinical risk factors should not automatically be considered contraindications to kidney transplantation. Surgical decisions should incorporate preoperative optimization, individualized risk–benefit assessment, and multidisciplinary evaluation to improve perioperative safety.

Keywords: Kidney transplantation; contraindications; preoperative assessment; anesthesiology; residual neuromuscular blockade.
