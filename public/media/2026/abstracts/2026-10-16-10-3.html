CLINICAL AND HEMODYNAMIC CHARACTERISTICS IN THE EARLY INTENSIVE CARE PERIOD AFTER ORTHOTOPIC HEART TRANSPLANTATION

ABSTRACT

In this single-center descriptive study, retrospective and prospective data were collected from 73 patients undergoing orthotopic heart transplantation at Viet Duc University Hospital. We evaluated the clinical, laboratory, and hemodynamic characteristics of recipients during the early postoperative period. The mean age was 38.33 ± 16.95 years, and 79.45% were male. Dilated cardiomyopathy was the leading indication for transplantation (76.71%). Left ventricular ejection fraction significantly improved from 26.18 ± 6.49% before transplantation to 67.58 ± 6.05% at discharge. During the first 72 postoperative hours, heart rate gradually decreased from 111.47 ± 17.90 to 96.15 ± 12.44 beats/min, while mean arterial pressure remained stable. Cardiac index remained elevated, and systemic vascular resistance index increased progressively, suggesting recovery from early postoperative vasoplegia. Lactate, liver enzymes, creatinine, and NT-proBNP levels decreased significantly, reflecting improved tissue perfusion and end-organ function. Early after heart transplantation, patients demonstrated stable hemodynamics, recovery of vascular tone, and preserved early graft function. Continuous monitoring of systemic vascular resistance and metabolic parameters may provide useful information for postoperative monitoring and patient management.

Keywords: heart transplantation; intensive care; hemodynamics; vasoplegia
