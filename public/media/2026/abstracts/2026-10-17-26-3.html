Launching a Stem Cell Transplantation unit with a special focus on Thalassemia in Vietnam at Hue Central Hospital

Hoa Thi Kim Nguyen, MD, PhD1,2, Hiep Nhu Pham, MD, PhD3, Tri Huu Pham, MD1, Lawrence Faulkner, MD4.

1Department of Hematology, Oncology, and Transplant, Pediatric Center, Hue Central Hospital, Vietnam.

2Pediatrics Department, School of Medicine and Pharmacy, Duy Tan University, Da Nang city, Vietnam.

3Oncology Center, Hue Central Hospital, Vietnam

4Cure2Children Foundation, Florence, Italy

 Abstract

Background: Thalassemia is highly prevalent in Vietnam, with approximately 2.000 new severe cases each year. Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) remains the standard long-term cure for severe thalassemia. This report describes the initial experience with HSCT for severe thalassemia at the Hue Central Hospital, Hue, Vietnam.

Materials and Methods: This was a prospective, interventional cohort study conducted at Hue Central Hospital, Vietnam, patients with severe thalassemia who received allogeneic stem cell transplantation between September 2024 (start of program) and March 2026. Data were analyzed using SPSS version18.0.

Results: A total of 15 matched-related BMTs were performed, all of whom are disease- and GVHD-free at the time of the analysis. The median age at HSCT was 6.5 years (2.0-10.0). The male to female ratio was 1.5 to 1. There were 10 cases with HbE/Beta-Thalassemia and 5 cases with Alpha-Thalassemia. The median pre-transplant ferritin level was 1107 ng/mL (236-2917). All patients received G-CSF-primed bone marrow with at a median dose of 9.79x108 nucleated cells/recipient kg (8.0-10x108). The median times to platelet and neutrophil engraftment were both 20 days. There were three cases (20%) with skin aGVHD grade I-II and four cases (26.7%) with hemorrhagic cystitis. The median chimerism on day 30 was 98.0%. The median Hb after transplant was 11.7 (9.2-14.1) g/dl. The direct out-of-pocket cost of transplantation was approximately US$ 5,000 per case, excluding hospital and professional charges, which were covered by the health insurance system in Vietnam. At last follow-up, all patients were alive, well, and transfusion independent.

Conclusions: Hue Central Hospital in Vietnam has established a feasible, highly effective, safe and reduced-cost matched sibling transplantation program for the cure of children with severe thalassemia.

Keywords: Allogeneic stem cell transplantation, severe thalassemia, HLA-matched siblings.
